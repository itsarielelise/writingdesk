## Why people give up

Most people who start a sourdough starter abandon it within a month. They read three blog posts that disagree about hydration percentages and feeding ratios, measure everything to the gram, and then panic the first time the jar smells like nail polish remover. The starter was fine; the anxiety killed it.

The other reason is that the instructions are written for bakers who bake every day. If you bake once a week, a routine that asks for two feedings a day at room temperature is a chore you will eventually skip, and then you will feel guilty, and then the jar will move to the back of the fridge forever.

## The simple routine

Keep the starter in the fridge. Once a week, take it out the night before you want to bake, throw away all but a spoonful, and feed it equal weights of flour and water. By morning it will be bubbly and ready. Use what you need and put the rest back in the fridge until next week.

That is the whole routine. It works because cold slows everything down, so a weekly feeding is plenty, and because discarding most of it each time keeps the culture young and active rather than letting acids build up.

If you want a really open crumb, use a high-hydration dough around eighty percent, do four sets of coil folds in the first two hours, and bake in a preheated cast iron pot with the lid on for the first twenty minutes.

## When things go wrong

A layer of grey liquid on top just means it is hungry. Pour it off or stir it in, then feed it. A sharp acetone smell means the same thing. Neither means the starter is dead, and both disappear after a feeding or two at room temperature.

The only real sign of trouble is fuzzy mould or pink and orange streaks. Then you throw it out and start again, which takes about a week. It happens to everyone eventually, and the second starter is always easier than the first.

Most starters that people throw away were perfectly healthy. The grey liquid and the solvent smell look alarming, but they only mean the culture needs feeding, and the people who give up are usually reacting to those signs rather than to anything actually wrong.
